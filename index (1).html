Brawl Stars Roast

A Totally Unserious Brawl Stars Roast

Disclaimer: This document is friendly gaming banter. It roasts certain playstyles, not every person who enjoys these brawlers.

1. Edgar Mains: The Super-Jump Scientists

Some Edgar mains see a full enemy team, leap straight into the middle of it, and act surprised when the respawn screen appears. Their entire strategy can look like:

  • Charge Super → jump in → get deleted.
  • Ignore three counters → blame randoms.
  • Miss the timing → call it lag.

Edgar himself isn't the problem. It's the player who treats every Super like a guaranteed victory instead of checking enemy health, gadgets, and positioning.

2. Kit Mains: The Cat Delivery Service

Some Kit mains spot a teammate and immediately decide that teamwork means becoming a backpack for the rest of the match. Others jump onto an enemy without checking whether the landing is safe, then wonder why the plan lasted half a second.

  • See teammate → attach instantly.
  • See enemy → pounce immediately.
  • Plan ahead → optional DLC.

Kit can be useful when played with timing and coordination. The roast is for players who press buttons first and think about the situation afterward.

3. The Shared Ranked Braincell

When a reckless Edgar and an equally reckless Kit queue together, the match becomes a live experiment in confidence exceeding strategy. One jumps in; the other follows; the kill feed starts writing the comedy.

4. Final Verdict

Edgar mains aren't automatically bad, and Kit mains aren't automatically clueless. But if you dive into a 1v3, ignore every warning sign, and blame your teammates afterward, the problem might not be your brawler. It might be your decision-making.

Play smart. Check the matchup. And please—look at the enemy team before jumping.
